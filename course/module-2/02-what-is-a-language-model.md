# Lesson 2.02 — What Is a Language Model?

## Try this first

Open ChatGPT. Type exactly this:

**"Complete this like a pirate: The sky is"**

What did you get? Something like "The sky be as blue as the deep sea, arrr!"?

Now open a new chat and type:

**"The sky is"**

Compare the two answers. Same start, totally different output.

---

## What changed?

You gave it a style. A character to play.

The AI shifted how it answered based on your instructions.

That ability — to adjust based on what you say — comes from training.

---

## What is a language model?

A **language model** is a program trained on a huge amount of text.

We're talking billions of web pages, books, articles, Wikipedia, code, and more.

During training, it read all of that and learned one thing: **what words tend to follow other words.**

---

## Like learning to speak by listening

Imagine you moved to a new country and didn't speak the language.

You watch TV for five years, read every book, listen to every conversation.

Eventually you'd pick up patterns: what words go together, how sentences are structured, what tone fits what situation.

That's basically what a language model does — except it does it with text, not sound.

---

## Why can it write like a pirate?

Because pirates exist in stories. The training data included pirate stories, pirate movies, pirate games.

The model learned: when someone says "like a pirate," certain words and phrases fit that vibe.

It's not acting. It's pattern-matching.

---

## The key idea

> A language model learned patterns from billions of sentences. It uses those patterns to predict what comes next.

It doesn't know what a pirate is. It just knows that pirate-sounding text looks a certain way.

---

## Activity

Try these in ChatGPT and see how the output changes:

- "Complete this like a sports announcer: The team ran onto the field and"
- "Complete this like a scientist: The team ran onto the field and"
- "Complete this like a five-year-old: The team ran onto the field and"

Same start. Different style. Totally different outputs.

**Pick your favorite one and paste it somewhere. You'll use it again later.**

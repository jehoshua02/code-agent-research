# Lesson 2.01 — AI Is Not Magic (It's Math)

## Try this first

Open ChatGPT. Type: **"The sky is"** and hit send.

What did it say? Something like "blue" or "the limit"?

Now try: **"The ocean is"**

Notice anything? The AI finished your sentence. Every time.

---

## So what just happened?

You might think: "Wow, it understood me."

But here's the truth: **it didn't understand anything.**

It predicted.

---

## Prediction, not thinking

Here's what AI is actually doing:

1. You type some words.
2. The AI looks at those words.
3. It asks: "What word comes next, most of the time?"
4. It picks that word.
5. Then it does it again. And again. And again.

That's it. No thinking. No understanding. Just: **what word usually comes next?**

---

## The autocomplete comparison

You know how your phone finishes your texts? Like you type "On my" and it suggests "way"?

AI is that — but trained on billions of sentences instead of just your texts.

It's not smarter than your phone. It just has way more data to predict from.

---

## Why does this matter?

Because if AI is just predicting words, then:

- It can sound confident and be completely wrong.
- It can make up things that never happened.
- It doesn't actually "know" anything the way you do.

You'll see all of this in action later in this module.

---

## The key idea

> AI doesn't think. It predicts the next most likely word, over and over.

That one sentence will explain almost everything weird you've ever seen AI do.

---

## Activity

Go to ChatGPT and try these prompts. Notice what happens:

- "Once upon a time there was a"
- "To make a peanut butter sandwich, first"
- "The capital of France is"

Each time, the AI is predicting — not remembering, not looking it up, not thinking.

Just predicting.

**Write down one thing that surprised you about the outputs.**

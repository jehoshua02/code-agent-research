# Lesson 2.09 — Hallucinations: When AI Makes Stuff Up

## Try this first

Ask ChatGPT:

**"Can you cite a source for that last answer? Give me a real article or book I can look up."**

Pick any previous answer to reference. Then Google the citation it gives you.

Does the article actually exist? Does the author name match? Is the quote real?

---

## AI lies. Not on purpose — but it does.

This is one of the most important things to understand about AI.

AI will sometimes state false things with total confidence.

It won't say "I'm not sure." It won't say "I'm guessing." It will just... say it. Like it's a fact.

This is called a **hallucination**.

---

## Why does it happen?

Remember: AI predicts words. It doesn't check facts.

When you ask for a source, the AI predicts what a real-sounding source looks like:
- An author name
- A publication
- A year
- A title

It generates something that looks correct. But it's assembled from patterns, not from a real database of sources.

The article might not exist. The author might not exist. The quote is probably invented.

---

## The confidence problem

The scariest part isn't that AI gets things wrong.

It's that it sounds exactly the same whether it's right or wrong.

There's no shake in the voice. No "I think, maybe." Just a clean, confident answer — real or fake.

This is why you can't trust AI for facts without checking.

---

## When does it happen most?

- Asking for specific sources, citations, or quotes
- Asking about niche or obscure topics
- Asking about recent events (past the training cutoff)
- Asking for statistics or numbers

For common, well-covered topics, AI is usually more reliable. But it can still slip.

---

## The key idea

> AI confidently makes things up because it's predicting words, not checking facts. This is called a hallucination.

Never trust AI output for facts without verifying it yourself.

---

## Activity

Ask ChatGPT:

**"Tell me about a scientific study that proves video games improve problem-solving skills. Give me the title, author, and journal."**

Then try to look up that study. Search the title. Search the author.

**Does it exist? What did you find?**

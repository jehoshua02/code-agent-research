# Lesson 2.05 — What Is a Prompt?

## Try this first

Open ChatGPT. Ask it:

**"Explain gravity."**

Read the answer.

Now open a new chat and ask:

**"Explain gravity like I'm 8 years old."**

Read that answer.

Same topic. Very different results. Why?

---

## Your words control everything

That difference happened because you changed your **prompt**.

A prompt is everything you send to an AI — your question, your instructions, your examples, your context.

The AI only knows what's in the prompt. It has no idea who you are, what you need, or how much you already know unless you tell it.

---

## Think of it like a search engine — but smarter

When you Google something, you type a few keywords and get links.

With AI, you can write full sentences. You can give instructions. You can say "use simple words" or "give me a list" or "be funny."

The AI will actually follow those instructions.

That's the power of prompting.

---

## What goes into a prompt?

A prompt can include:

- **A question** — "What is photosynthesis?"
- **Instructions** — "Explain it simply" or "Use bullet points"
- **Context** — "I'm writing a report for 7th grade science class"
- **Examples** — "Like this: [your example]"
- **A role** — "You are a friendly tutor"

You don't have to include all of these. But the more useful info you give, the better the output.

---

## Garbage in, garbage out

If you give a vague prompt, you get a vague answer.

Compare:
- "Tell me about dogs" — could be about anything
- "Give me 5 fun facts about golden retrievers for a 6th grade presentation" — very specific, very useful

The second prompt takes 10 more seconds to write and saves you 5 minutes of editing.

---

## The key idea

> A prompt is everything you send to the AI. Better prompts = better answers.

Learning to write good prompts is one of the most useful skills in this course.

---

## Activity

Pick any topic you like. Ask ChatGPT about it in two ways:

1. A vague one-word or two-word prompt.
2. A detailed prompt with instructions, context, and a format request.

**Compare the two answers. Which one was actually useful? What made the difference?**

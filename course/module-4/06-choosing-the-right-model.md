# Lesson 4.06 — Choosing the Right Model

## The Big Idea

Not all AI models are the same. They trade off three things: speed, cost, and intelligence.

Using the most powerful model for every task is like driving a semi-truck to pick up one pizza. It works, but it's overkill.

Knowing which model to reach for makes you faster and smarter.

---

## The Three Tradeoffs

**Speed vs. Intelligence**
Smaller models respond in under a second. Bigger models think harder but take longer. For a quick autocomplete, fast is better. For a complex architecture question, you want the smarter model.

**Cost vs. Capability**
Free models are surprisingly good now. You don't need to pay for most everyday tasks. Save the expensive model for when it actually matters.

**Context length vs. Price**
Some models can read huge amounts of text at once. That's useful for long code files. But it costs more. Match the model to the task.

---

## A Simple Mental Model

Think of models like tools in a toolbox:

- **Screwdriver** (fast, cheap model) — use it constantly, for small stuff
- **Drill** (mid-tier model) — your go-to for real work
- **Power saw** (top model) — breaks out when the job actually needs it

Most of your tasks are screwdriver tasks. Don't reach for the power saw out of habit.

---

## What This Looks Like in Practice

- Explaining a concept → cheap fast model
- Writing a full feature → mid-tier model
- Debugging a tricky, mysterious bug → top model
- Generating a quick list → cheapest thing available
- Reviewing architecture decisions → top model

---

## Key Takeaway

There's no single "best" model. There's the right model for the job. Match the size of the tool to the size of the task.

As you build more, this becomes instinct. For now, start by noticing: am I using a semi-truck when I need a bike?

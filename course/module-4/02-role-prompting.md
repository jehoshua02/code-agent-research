# Lesson 4.02 — Role Prompting: Give the AI a Job

## The Big Idea

Imagine asking two different people to review your essay — your English teacher and your best friend.

Same essay. Totally different feedback.

AI works the same way. Tell it who to be, and it changes how it talks, what it focuses on, and how deep it goes.

---

## Do This First

Paste any code you've written into ChatGPT or Claude. Then ask:

**Version 1:**
> "Review this code. You are a senior developer who cares about best practices and clean code."

**Version 2:**
> "Review this code. You are a teacher explaining things to a beginner who just started coding."

Run both. Read both. Notice how different they feel.

---

## What Just Happened

The AI didn't get a different brain. You gave it a different job.

The senior developer version probably:
- Pointed out edge cases
- Suggested more professional patterns
- Used technical terms

The beginner teacher version probably:
- Explained what each part does
- Used simpler words
- Encouraged you

Same code. Same AI. Completely different output — because you changed the role.

---

## Why Roles Work

The AI has seen millions of examples of how a "senior developer" talks and what they care about. When you say those words, it pulls from that pattern.

It's like choosing a character in a video game. Same world, but different stats, different moves, different style.

---

## Roles You Can Actually Use

| Role | When to use it |
|------|----------------|
| Senior developer | When you want strict, clean-code feedback |
| Beginner teacher | When you want simple explanations |
| Technical writer | When you want clear documentation |
| Security expert | When you want to find vulnerabilities |
| Product manager | When you want to think about users, not code |

---

## Key Takeaway

The role you give the AI shapes everything. When output feels off — too complicated, too basic, wrong tone — try changing the role first.

It's the fastest way to fix a bad response without rewriting the whole prompt.

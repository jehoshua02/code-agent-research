# Lesson 4.12 — Final Project: Spec It Out

## The Big Idea

Before you build, you write a spec.

A **spec** (short for specification) is a short document that describes what your app does. Not the code — just a clear description of how the app works from the user's point of view.

Developers write specs before building because once you start coding, it's easy to lose track of what you're actually supposed to be making. The spec is your anchor.

---

## Do This First

Write a spec for your app. It should fit on one page. Use this structure:

---

**App Name:** [Your app name]

**What it does:**
One or two sentences. Plain language. What does the user do? What does the app do back?

**Who uses it:**
One sentence. Who is this for?

**Pages / Screens:**
List each screen in your app. Give each a name and a one-line description.

- Home — [what the user sees here]
- [Next screen] — [what happens here]
- ...

**Key interactions:**
What happens when you click things? List the main actions:

- When the user clicks [button], [what happens]
- When the user types in [field], [what happens]
- ...

**What it does NOT do:**
List 2-3 things that are out of scope. This prevents scope creep.

---

Write that now. Rough is fine. It will change as you build.

---

## Why a Spec Saves Time

Imagine you ask AI to build your app with no spec. You just describe it loosely. The AI guesses about the parts you didn't explain.

Then you see the result and say "that's not what I meant." You go back and forth correcting it. Each correction takes time.

A spec means your prompt is already 80% of the way there. The AI has a clear target.

---

## The Scope Creep Problem

Scope creep is when your project keeps growing.

You start with "a simple to-do app." Then you add dark mode. Then multiple lists. Then sharing. Then notifications. Then you never finish.

The "does NOT do" section of your spec is how you fight this. Write it down and stick to it. You can always add features after the first version is done.

---

## Your Spec is a Living Document

It will change. That's fine.

When you build something and realize a screen should work differently, update the spec. When you cut a feature, remove it. The spec should always reflect what you're actually building right now.

---

## Key Takeaway

Write a one-page spec before building. List your screens and what happens when you click things. Write what the app does NOT do. This document becomes your first AI prompt — and your guard against building forever without finishing.
